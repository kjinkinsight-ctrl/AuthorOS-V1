# Author Studio Market Research & Competitive Comparison

## 1) Executive summary

Author Studio is best positioned as a hybrid writing workspace for authors who want to combine drafting, storyline structure, continuity checking, and project management in one application.

From the product structure, the app is not trying to be a pure word processor like Google Docs or a pure plotting tool like Plottr. Instead, it blends:

- manuscript drafting
- timeline-based story planning
- scene/beat planning
- continuity warnings and timeline validation
- reading-rhythm and writing-session guidance
- export support for manuscript workflows
- backup/sync health and project-level data retention

This puts Author Studio in the "authors' creative studio" category, closer to Scrivener + Plottr + Aeon Timeline, but with a more opinionated focus on narrative continuity and author workflow intelligence.

The strongest opportunity is to own a middle ground between:

- heavy, complex writing suites that are more general but less focused on story consistency
- specialist plotting tools that are excellent for outlines but weaker for drafting
- AI writing platforms that add productivity but often dilute craft-focused workflows

## 2) What Author Studio appears to be

Based on the app structure and feature set, the product is aimed at:

- fiction writers
- long-form authors
- fantasy / romance / thriller / multi-POV storytellers
- authors who need structure without losing drafting momentum
- users who care about visual planning, character continuity, and timeline coherence

Core product signals from the code and UI:

- manuscript-centric writing environment
- timeline and era/sequence modeling
- visual planning board for scenes and arcs
- continuity analyzer to detect character overlap, impossible travel, invalid ranges, missing POV presence, etc.
- reading rhythm presets for writing cadence
- project backup and health monitoring
- manuscript export presets for publishing/printing workflows
- local persistence plus cloud-ready sync architecture
- strong author-facing brand language such as "Write with structure, continuity, and momentum"

This makes Author Studio feel like a modern, narrative-focused writing toolkit rather than a generic notes app or simple writing app.

## 3) Competitive landscape

### 3.1 Scrivener

Website: https://www.literatureandlatte.com/scrivener

Primary positioning:
- The long-form writing system for authors, screenwriters, academics, journalists, and researchers.
- Strong emphasis on section-based writing, project organization, research in reach, and manuscript compiles.

Strengths:
- Mature project organization model
- Excellent for writing large documents without losing structure
- Outstanding for research-heavy writing and manuscript assembly
- Familiar binder/outline metaphor
- Powerful compile/export tooling
- Strong long-form drafting workflow for nonfiction and fiction

Weaknesses vs. Author Studio:
- More general writing workstation than narrative continuity system
- Less tailored to story continuity validation and character-level plotting intelligence
- Can feel heavier and more complex for writers who want a cleaner, more focused author workspace

Competitive takeaway:
- Scrivener is the benchmark for flexibility and professional long-form writing workflow.
- Author Studio can position itself as a lighter, more narrative-intelligent author environment with a stronger story-planning lens.

### 3.2 Plottr

Website: https://www.plottr.com

Primary positioning:
- Visual book outlining and story bible software.
- Focused on fast plotting, scene cards, templates, and series planning.

Strengths:
- Excellent visual planning experience
- Strong support for plot templates and genre structures
- Good scene-level planning and series-level storytelling organization
- Very strong positioning for plotters and outlining-first writers
- Large marketing focus on book outlining, character sheets, and world-building

Weaknesses vs. Author Studio:
- More planning-first than drafting-first
- Typically not a full writing environment in the same way a manuscript workspace is
- Export is useful, but the product is not designed to feel like a full author studio
- Less emphasis on continuity analysis than a project that tracks timeline validity and POV integrity

Competitive takeaway:
- Plottr wins on speed and clarity of story planning.
- Author Studio should compete by combining Plottr's clarity with manuscript depth and continuity intelligence.

### 3.3 Novelcrafter

Website: https://www.novelcrafter.com

Primary positioning:
- Writing platform for authors with planning, codex/world-building, AI assistance, collaboration, and manuscript tools.

Strengths:
- Strong world-building and codex functionality
- AI-enabled brainstorming, review, and writing support
- Multi-genre flexibility
- Collaboration and team-sharing features
- Good blend of writing and planning for modern authors

Weaknesses vs. Author Studio:
- Heavily shaped by AI-driven author workflows
- More general software ecosystem than a simple, focused writing workstation
- May feel less elegant for writers who want a clean, narrative-first tool without AI-centrality
- Continuity validation is less explicit than a dedicated timeline and story integrity system

Competitive takeaway:
- Novelcrafter is strongest when it leans into AI-assisted productivity and worldbuilding.
- Author Studio can differentiate by emphasizing craft discipline, structure, continuity, and less AI-heavy workflow clarity.

### 3.4 Aeon Timeline

Website: https://www.aeontimeline.com

Primary positioning:
- Timeline-first story and project planning tool for complex narratives and project management.

Strengths:
- Robust timeline planning for large and complex story systems
- Excellent for chronology, dependencies, and plot visualization
- Great for narrative planning and project structure clarity
- Strong for authors managing many moving parts over time

Weaknesses vs. Author Studio:
- Better as a planning layer than a writing layer
- Less directly connected to the draft-writing workflow
- More analytical and less creative/immersive in feel
- Not built as a manuscript workspace with writing momentum tools

Competitive takeaway:
- Aeon Timeline is a planning specialist, not a drafting studio.
- Author Studio’s advantage is that it combines timeline analytics with live drafting and continuity alerts.

### 3.5 Google Docs / general writing tools

Primary positioning:
- Collaboration, document drafting, standard writing workflow, low friction, widely adopted.

Strengths:
- Ubiquitous and easy to adopt
- Great for collaboration
- Strong editing workflow
- Fast for simple drafting

Weaknesses vs. Author Studio:
- Weak for long-form story planning and continuity management
- Minimal support for narrative structure, plotline logic, or visual outlines
- Not designed for author-specific worldbuilding or writing systems

Competitive takeaway:
- Author Studio is not trying to compete as a general word processor.
- It competes as a creative writing system for authors with structural needs beyond simple drafting.

## 5) Direct comparison: where Author Studio is differentiated

### 5.1 Unique strengths

Author Studio stands out most in four areas:

1. Continuity intelligence
   - The app includes explicit continuity warnings for invalid ranges, impossible travel, overlapping character presence, missing POV presence, and timeline ordering issues.
   - This is more than a visual outline; it is active story integrity validation.

2. Narrative workflow integration
   - The product combines manuscript writing, chapter planning, timeline analysis, and visual story structure in one authored workflow.
   - This reduces the mental switching cost between drafting and planning.

3. Author-specific UX language
   - The experience speaks to writing momentum, continuity, rhythm, and creative clarity rather than generic productivity.
   - The app is not just a planner; it is a writer's studio.

4. Creative-system feel
   - The design language and structure suggest a product built for deep, sustained fiction work rather than a lightweight note-taking tool.

### 5.2 Where it is currently less mature than category leaders

Author Studio likely trails category leaders in these areas:

- established ecosystem and community
- pricing model and commercial polish
- mature collaboration workflows
- desktop/mobile cross-platform depth
- export polish and manuscript workflow maturity
- large third-party integrations
- built-in onboarding and user education

This is normal for a new or early-stage author tool. The product is already stronger in narrative intelligence and author workflow integration than many competitors, but it still needs to become more productized and enterprise-proof.

## 7) Competitive gaps and opportunities

### Opportunity: "story integrity" as a category

The strongest opportunity is to own a message around "story integrity" rather than only "outlining".

The app already has the underlying logic for:

- continuity warnings
- timeline ordering checks
- character overlap validation
- travel logic and POV consistency

This is a meaningful differentiator because most story tools focus on structure, not on the rules of narrative coherence.

### Opportunity: author workflow + structure, not just writing or plotting

The product is strongest when marketed as the place where the writer can:

- think in scenes
- draft the manuscript
- validate continuity
- track timeline and plotline arcs
- review rhythm and momentum
- export when ready

This combination is powerful and highly specific.

### Opportunity: less AI, more craft

The app seems intentionally less AI-heavy than some competitors. That is a strategic advantage if the target user is a craft-focused author who values attention, clarity, and authorship over generic automation.

## 8) Recommended product direction

To compete effectively, Author Studio should focus on six product moves that turn its existing strengths into a clearer market advantage:

1. Sharpen the narrative differentiation
   - Position continuity intelligence as a flagship feature, not a secondary utility.
   - Make story integrity, timeline validation, and character consistency core to the product narrative.

2. Improve user education
   - Add quick-start templates, guided writing workflows, and onboarding tutorial flows for new authors.
   - Reduce the learning curve so first-time users can understand the app's narrative system quickly.

3. Strengthen manuscript export quality
   - Upgrade manuscript presentation, compile layouts, and publishing exports to feel polished and premium.
   - Treat export quality as part of the product's credibility, not just a utility feature.

4. Expand collaboration and sharing
   - Introduce editor and beta-reader workflows while preserving the local-author experience.
   - Enable sharing and review without making the tool feel like a generic team document platform.

5. Add stronger worldbuilding and character database depth
   - Expand the story bible layer with deeper character records, relationship tracking, and world-building tools.
   - Position the app as a serious creative reference system as well as a drafting environment.

6. Make the visual planning layer feel as polished as the drafting layer
   - Elevate the story board so it feels premium, fluid, and visually intentional rather than utility-only.
   - Ensure planning, timeline, and drafting all deliver a consistent author-grade experience.

## 9) Bottom line

Author Studio is positioned as a compelling middle-ground writer tool:

- more structured than a normal writing app
- more narrative-aware than a typical plotting tool
- more focused and author-centric than broad AI writing platforms
- deeper in continuity validation than many story planning competitors

If the team continues to sharpen its storytelling-specific differentiation and polish the user experience around manuscript + planning + continuity, it can carve out a clear niche for serious fiction writers who want craft-quality structure without the bloat of a general-purpose writing suite.

## 10) Visual design comparison and feedback

### 10.1 Visual comparison against competitors

#### Author Studio
Your app has a strong editorial, warm-literary aesthetic. The visual language is anchored by:

- deep navy / charcoal backgrounds
- warm parchment and paper-inspired light theme
- gold / brass accents for emphasis
- dense information panels with a desktop workspace feeling
- a kind of serious author-studio vibe rather than a consumer SaaS vibe

This is a good fit for a fiction-focused tool because it feels more like a creative workspace than a generic productivity app. The dark themes especially feel premium and immersive, with enough contrast to support long writing sessions.

#### Scrivener
Scrivener’s visual style is more utilitarian and practical than emotionally atmospheric. It feels like a professional writing workstation built around organization and production. The design is less about "brand personality" and more about creative function: binder, cards, outlines, research panels, and editing surfaces.

Compared to your app:
- Scrivener is more austere and workmanlike
- Author Studio is warmer and more branded/immersive
- Scrivener leans functional; Author Studio leans editorial and mood-driven

#### Plottr
Plottr is probably the closest visual match in terms of storytelling-first presentation. It uses:

- colorful visualization
- scene-card logic
- plotline highlighting
- a more diagrammatic, outline-driven layout

Comparison:
- Plottr feels more playful and schematized
- Author Studio feels more mature and studio-like
- Plottr is optimized for visual clarity in plotting; your app is optimized for deeper author workflow immersion

#### Novelcrafter
Novelcrafter is visually modern, web-native, and more polished in the SaaS sense. Its UI tends to feel:

- cleaner
- lighter in density
- more productized
- built around modular panels and flexible workflows

Compared to your app:
- Novelcrafter feels lighter and more contemporary
- Author Studio feels more atmospheric and denser
- Your app may have more personality, but it also risks feeling heavier or more crowded at first glance

#### Aeon Timeline
Aeon Timeline is the most analytical in visual style. It prioritizes structure, chronology, and clarity over aesthetic warmth. It is designed to communicate data and relationships more than mood.

Compared to your app:
- Aeon Timeline is cleaner and more data-centric
- your app is more emotionally resonant and creative
- your app has stronger brand identity, but slightly less visual simplicity

### 10.2 What is working visually

1. Strong theme personality
   - The color system is memorable and appropriate for a writing app.
   - The warm paper and dark literary palettes make the app feel intentionally creative.

2. Good author-brand fit
   - The app does not feel like a bland productivity tool.
   - It feels more like a narrative studio or writing desk.

3. Good use of accent color
   - Gold, brass, teal, and deeper tones give the interface depth and signal hierarchy.

4. Clear “tooling” impression
   - The desktop layout communicates that this is a serious workspace with multiple systems inside one app.

5. Distinct behavioral identity
   - The dark and warm palette combinations help the app feel original, which is a real advantage in a crowded author-tool market.

### 10.3 What could be improved visually

1. Reduce density in the main workspace
   - The interface currently feels rich and full, but some surfaces may be visually busy.
   - For long writing sessions, users often prefer calmer panels and more breathing room.

2. Increase consistency between the writing area and the planning area
   - The app clearly has multiple modes, but the transitions between manuscript, timeline, planning, and dashboard could use stronger visual continuity.
   - The difference between sections should feel intentional, not accidental.

3. Make the primary hierarchy stronger
   - The app has a lot of information and several action surfaces. Some calls to action compete visually.
   - Consider more differentiation between:
     - primary author actions
     - secondary utilities
     - informational badges and status widgets

4. Refine the “desktop professional” feel
   - The app has good depth and weight, but some panels may feel more like a control center than a creative writing environment.
   - A more guided visual rhythm would help the app feel more intentional and less overloaded.

5. Consider more whitespace around key writing surfaces
   - For creative work, breathing room is not wasted space; it protects focus.
   - The app could benefit from slightly more compact but calmer panel spacing.

### 10.4 Best visual direction for the product

The strongest visual direction for Author Studio is:

- premium literary workspace
- warm and immersive but structured
- creative, not playful
- practical, not sterile

The app should aim for a balance between:

- Scrivener’s functional seriousness
- Plottr’s story-driven visual clarity
- Novelcrafter’s sleek product polish

In other words, your app should feel like a high-end writing studio, not a generic dashboard and not a toy-like plotting tool.

### 10.5 Specific UI feedback

1. Keep the current warm literary palette, but tune it for less visual noise
   - The dark themes are strong; the light paper theme is especially compelling.
   - Use the accent colors more surgically so they highlight important actions without creating visual competition.

2. Improve focus by separating composition from system controls
   - The writing experience should feel quieter than the planning tools.
   - The editor should visually recede when the user is drafting, and the structure tools should become more visible when needed.

3. Use stronger card hierarchy and panel rhythm
   - Cards and panels should have more deliberate spacing and less equal emphasis.
   - The app currently has many rich surfaces; organizing them into primary, secondary, and tertiary importance would help.

4. Make navigation feel lighter on desktop
   - The top bar works, but a more reduced navigation density could help the product feel less like a control room and more like a studio.

5. Keep the atmosphere, but add a clearer “premium SaaS polish” layer
   - This would help your app feel more competitive with products like Novelcrafter without losing its unique creative identity.

### 10.6 Summary of the visual assessment

Your app already has a strong foundation:

- strong aesthetic personality
- good creative tone
- valid author-focused positioning
- memorable theme system

The main next step is not to become more generic; it is to become more refined.

The ideal version of the app would keep the literary warmth and serious tone, while reducing visual clutter, sharpening hierarchy, and making the writing and planning surfaces feel more intentionally separated.

That would make the product feel both more premium and more usable.

## 11) Sellability comparison and suggestions

### 11.1 How sellable is Author Studio versus the market?

#### Overall sellability: good potential, but still needs stronger positioning and polish

Author Studio has a promising commercial story because it combines several very attractive features in one niche product:

- writing workflow
- structure and plotting
- continuity validation
- world-building and project management
- export-ready manuscript structure

This is a strong value proposition for fiction writers and serious authors. In market terms, it is more compelling than a generic notebook or a single-purpose brainstorming tool, but it still has to prove that it is easier, more special, or more useful than the more established products already in the space.

#### Compared with Scrivener
Scrivener is highly sellable because it is already the name many serious writers know. Its value is strong because it solves a real pain point: handling big writing projects without losing structure. It is also broad in usefulness across nonfiction, fiction, and research-heavy work.

Author Studio is not as universally known, but it can still sell to a narrower, more targeted audience: authors who want narrative structure, continuity safety, and creative workflow clarity.

Sellability verdict:
- Scrivener wins on brand recognition and mature trust.
- Author Studio wins on niche story-focused differentiation.

#### Compared with Plottr
Plottr is very sellable because its message is extremely easy to understand: "plan your book faster and visually." It offers a crisp transformation story and a highly marketable promise.

Author Studio’s message is more complex but also more interesting: "write, organize, validate, and sustain narrative continuity." This is a stronger long-term positioning for creative professionals, but it needs cleaner messaging to sell quickly.

Sellability verdict:
- Plottr wins on clarity and marketing simplicity.
- Author Studio wins on depth and creative-system ambition.

#### Compared with Novelcrafter
Novelcrafter is highly sellable in a modern SaaS environment because it has a strong, contemporary product story: planning, writing, AI access, and collaboration in one place. It feels current and scalable.

Author Studio may be less broad in reach, but it can still sell well to authors who dislike AI-first workflows and want a disciplined craft-focused experience.

Sellability verdict:
- Novelcrafter wins on modern product positioning and ecosystem appeal.
- Author Studio wins on craft focus and a more premium author-studio identity.

#### Compared with Aeon Timeline
Aeon Timeline sells because it solves a very specific and high-value pain point: timeline complexity. It is strong in credibility and technical usefulness, especially for planners and researchers.

Author Studio is less purely technical and more creative, which gives it a stronger emotional appeal to fiction writers, but it also means it has to work harder to explain the product's value in plain language.

Sellability verdict:
- Aeon Timeline wins on clarity of use case.
- Author Studio wins on emotional appeal and author identity.

### 11.2 Where Author Studio is strongest in sellability

1. Clear niche
   - It is not trying to be everything for everyone.
   - The product speaks directly to authors who care about structure, continuity, and creative flow.

2. Premium creative identity
   - The app feels like a serious author workspace, which is a strong product story.

3. Unique story-validation logic
   - The continuity analyzer is a concrete differentiator that many writing apps do not offer in a comparable way.

4. Stronger emotional story than a spreadsheet-like planner
   - It sells as a place to write and think creatively, not just plan.

### 11.3 Where sellability is weak

1. Product message is still too layered
   - The app wants to be many things at once, and that can blur the pitch.
   - Buyers need a very simple headline and a very specific promise.

2. Product polish still reads as early-stage
   - The product has substance, but it has a less polished commercial feel than the strongest SaaS products in this category.

3. The value is not always obvious at first glance
   - A user may understand the manuscript and timeline features, but not immediately grasp the deeper continuity and author-system benefit.

4. Limited proof of traction or social proof
   - This is a marketability issue, not necessarily a product issue.
   - Brands in this category sell better when they show a community, tutorials, testimonials, and strong usage signals.

### 11.4 Suggestions to improve sellability

#### 1. Simplify the main product promise
Create a headline that is shorter, sharper, and easier to understand.

Good examples:
- Write with structure, continuity, and momentum.
- Plan the story. Draft the book. Protect the timeline.
- A writing studio for authors who need both craft and continuity.

This should be repeated consistently across landing pages, app onboarding, store listings, and demo videos.

#### 2. Build a stronger feature ladder
Users need to understand what is high-value immediately.

Suggested order:
- Write
- Plan scenes
- Track story arcs
- Check continuity
- Export manuscript
- Review performance and rhythm

The app should present features in a simple progression rather than as a dense menu of tools.

#### 3. Make the continuity feature a flagship, not a secondary tool
This is one of the strongest differentiators. It should be visible in marketing and onboarding.

Examples:
- "Continuity checks for POV, timeline, plot overlap, and travel logic"
- "Catch story problems before they become rewrite problems"

This is one of the easiest ways to differentiate from Plottr and Scrivener.

#### 4. Invest in onboarding and first-run education
The app should help new users understand the product in 3 minutes.

Possible steps:
- create a starter project template
- show how a manuscript flows into timeline planning
- explain the continuity dashboard
- show one example of a timeline warning and how to fix it

Users need to feel that the product is immediately valuable.

#### 5. Create a more polished product story for outbound marketing
This should include:
- clear app screenshots
- short 30-60 second demo videos
- before/after examples of timeline continuity issues
- screenshots of the writing experience and planning view
- author testimonials

The product already has the depth; it needs more material that sells the depth cleanly.

#### 6. Align the product with a specific user persona
The product is strongest when it targets a clear writer persona.

Examples:
- fiction authors with multi-POV stories
- fantasy and romance writers with complex timelines
- authors who want plot structure without sacrificing drafting momentum
- multi-book or series writers who need continuity and timeline tracking

This is more marketable than trying to serve every author equally.

#### 7. Clarify why users should switch from existing tools
The app should tell a direct story like:
- Plottr helps you plan, but Author Studio helps you draft and validate the story in the same environment.
- Scrivener helps you organize, but Author Studio helps you track story integrity while writing.
- Google Docs helps you write, but Author Studio helps you keep your story coherent.

This is highly useful in app-store listings, demos, and sales conversations.

### 11.5 Best commercialization direction

Author Studio should not pitch itself as a broad writing platform. It should pitch itself as a focused author operating system for narrative integrity.

The strongest marketable formula is:

- writing studio
- planning system
- continuity engine
- story bible
- manuscript export

This gives the product a memorable pitch and a differentiated niche.

### 11.6 Final verdict on sellability

Author Studio is a promising product with genuine differentiation, but it currently sells best to users who already understand the value of structure, planning, and continuity in fiction writing. To scale beyond that, it needs:

- sharper positioning
- cleaner messaging
- more polished onboarding
- stronger proof and social trust
- a more cohesive story around why it is better than plot-only or writing-only tools

If those improvements are made, the app has a credible path to competing effectively in the serious-author software space.

## 13) Phase 1 feature build roadmap

The best next move is to build the four features below in order. This creates a product story that is easier to explain, easier to sell, and more valuable to authors from day one.

### 13.1 Starter templates

Why this is first:
- It solves the blank-page problem immediately.
- It makes adoption easier for new users.
- It gives the product a clearer onboarding story and a higher conversion rate.

Feature goal:
- let a user create a project from a template instead of an empty workspace

Recommended templates:
- Romance
- Thriller
- Fantasy epic
- Mystery / suspense
- Literary fiction
- Series / multi-book project

What to include in each template:
- chapter structure suggestions
- core plot beat stages
- starting character list
- working POV suggestions
- initial story arc and timeline placeholders
- optional notes about theme and ending structure

Build plan:
1. Create a new-project wizard with template selection.
2. Each template generates a starter timeline, chapter list, and scene structure.
3. Add a visible “Use this template” flow in the onboarding screen and new project screen.
4. Allow users to convert template beats into custom project elements afterward.
5. Add light editing controls so users can delete, rename, or reorder generated sections.

Success criteria:
- a new user can create a working story project in under 2 minutes
- the template output feels inspiring rather than generic
- the project is immediately usable without additional setup

Implementation kickoff for Starter Templates:
1. Add a `ProjectTemplate` model with fields for `id`, `name`, `description`, `genre`, `chapterPlan`, `scenePlan`, `goal`, and `starterCharacterNames`.
2. Add a `ProjectTemplateStore` with a static set of built-in templates and optional future loading from JSON.
3. Add a `NewProjectDialog` or onboarding flow that presents template cards with preview text and a single action: `Use Template`.
4. On template selection, generate a starter `StarterProject` with chapters, scenes, and initial character placeholders.
5. Attach the template data to the existing project creation flow so the user can edit or replace the generated structure right away.
6. Keep the first version limited to 3-5 templates to avoid a huge design and content burden.

Priority:
- highest for product adoption and early traction

### 13.2 Story Codex / world memory system

Why this is second:
- this is the clearest long-term retention feature
- it turns the app from a writing tool into a story system
- it strengthens continuity checks by giving the app a central source of truth

Feature goal:
- create a shared database of all important story entities, facts, and relationships

Core entity types:
- characters
- places
- factions / organizations
- artifacts / magic systems / objects
- lore entries / backstory blocks
- relationships and aliases

Data model to add:
- id
- name
- category
- aliases
- short description
- tags
- linked scenes
- linked timeline events
- notes
- relationship links
- mention count

Core UI:
- Codex sidebar or panel with searchable records
- record detail view with tags, aliases, and linked scenes
- relationship graph or relationship list
- quick filter by category, tags, and usage

Build plan:
1. Create a project-level Codex store and models for characters, worlds, lore, and locations.
2. Add a record list view searchable by name and tag.
3. Add a detail view with notes, links, aliases, and mention references.
4. Link Codex entries to scenes and timeline events.
5. Add mention tracking so the app can show where a character, place, or item appears across scenes.
6. Connect the Codex to continuity analysis so warnings can be based on known world facts

Success criteria:
- users can create and search world elements without leaving the workspace
- a timeline event can link to character, place, or lore references
- Codex data improves continuity quality and reduces story mistakes

Priority:
- highest strategic feature for differentiation and retention

### 13.3 Research-in-reach panel

Why this is third:
- it makes the app much more usable for difficult long-form writing and worldbuilding-heavy projects
- it directly addresses a major pain point from the Scrivener comparison

Feature goal:
- keep research, notes, and scene context visible without interrupting the writing flow

Core behaviors:
- split workspace with manuscript on one side and reference panel on the other
- notes, character summaries, timeline context, world facts, and research snippets can all live beside the editor
- quick references can be pinned during writing sessions

Recommended panel types:
- Notes
- Research
- World facts
- Timeline context
- Character summaries

Build plan:
1. Add a layout toggle for focused writing vs. split workspace.
2. Add a reference panel attached to the manuscript editor with tabs and quick filtering.
3. Allow users to pin references from any project area into the panel.
4. Add lightweight document linking from scenes to notes or world records.
5. Give the panel a search function with direct jump links to the relevant record.

UX direction:
- the editor should remain the priority surface
- the reference panel should feel secondary, supportive, and unobtrusive
- users should be able to collapse or widen it without losing their position

Success criteria:
- writers can keep notes or references open while drafting without breaking rhythm

### 13.4 Advanced timeline metadata

Why this is fourth:
- it gives the app the depth needed for complex stories, series, and long arcs
- it is a strong differentiator from simpler plotting apps and supports serious author workflows

Feature goal:
- transform the timeline from a sequence of events into a rich narrative data layer

Add these fields to timeline events:
- custom tags
- story status
- POV focus
- plotline
- relationship notes
- chapter/arc association
- linked item references
- custom metadata fields
- location and travel notes

Recommended views:
- event detail panel
- plotline filter view
- character focus view
- relationship matrix or grouped dependency view
- timeline event metadata summary

Build plan:
1. Expand the existing event model with custom metadata fields.
2. Add a metadata editor to each timeline event.
3. Support tags and filters by character, location, status, and plotline.
4. Create a filtered relationship view for character overlap, scene dependencies, and arc continuity.
5. Surface relevant metadata in the editor and planning board so the timeline is not isolated from the writing workflow.

Success criteria:
- a user can attach story context to an event without leaving the timeline view
- timeline data becomes useful for continuity checks, plot analysis, and long-form planning
- the timeline feels like a meaningful analytical layer, not just a calendar

Priority:
- medium-high
- especially valuable for series writers, multi-POV stories, and complex narrative structures

2. Story Codex / world memory
3. Research-in-reach panel
